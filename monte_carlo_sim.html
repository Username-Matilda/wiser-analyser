Monte Carlo Simulation Tool

Transform uncertainty into probability distributions

The Essence: Instead of asking "what's the worst case?", we ask "what's the probability distribution?" Monte Carlo reveals not just what could happen, but how likely each outcome truly is.

When to use this: Any time you're planning something with multiple uncertainties (project costs, timelines, investment returns, risk assessment). If you've ever made a spreadsheet with "best/worst/likely" scenarios, this tool shows you everything in between - and how probable each outcome actually is.
🎯 Quick Start Guide:
  1. Define your uncertain variables (use full numbers - write 50000, not "50k")
  2. Choose how each variable behaves (distribution type)
  3. Write a formula combining your variables
  4. Run 10,000 simulations to see a broad range of possible futures

Step 1: Define Variables

💡 Naming tip: Use descriptive labels! Instead of "V1", write "Monthly Revenue" or "Project Duration". Always use full numbers (50000 not 50k) - this makes results clearer.

Step 2: Build Your Formula

The Art of Formula Design: Your formula reveals how uncertainties interact. Consider: Do your variables multiply (compounding uncertainty)? Add (independent risks)? The formula encodes your understanding of how reality's pieces fit together.
🔍 Formula guidance:
• Simple addition: V1 + V2 (independent costs)
• Multiplication: V1 * V2 (duration × rate)
• With contingency: (V1 * V2) * (1 + V3) where V3 is a risk buffer (0-50%)
• Complex interactions: (V1 * V2) + V3 - (V1 * V2 * V4)
• Functions: sqrt(V1), max(V1, V2), min(V1, 1000000)

Use variable names (V1, V2, etc.) with operators: +, -, *, /, ^
