Cone of Possibilities

The future branches on our assumptions. Make them explicit.

What this tool does: The Cone of Possibilities helps you explore how the future might unfold depending on which assumptions hold true. Rather than predicting a single outcome, you map out a structured range of scenarios — from baseline expectations to plausible alternatives to low-probability wildcards.

The core insight: Most organizations operate with an implicit "official future" — a set of shared assumptions about what will happen. The Cone makes these assumptions explicit and systematically varies them, revealing the branching structure of possibility. The cone widens as time extends: near-term futures are more constrained, while distant futures branch into wider possibility space.

How it works:

  • Identify key drivers — the forces that will shape your situation
  • State baseline assumptions — your current best guess for each driver
  • Vary the assumptions — create alternative scenarios by changing 1-2 drivers (plausible) or making more extreme shifts (wildcard)
  • Explore the cone — visualize your scenarios and consider how to anticipate, plan, and respond

Example: AI Development Trajectories

Question: "How might the AI capabilities landscape evolve over the next 2 years?"

Drivers: Compute scaling trends, algorithmic breakthroughs, regulatory response, lab coordination dynamics

Baseline: Compute costs decline 30%/year, incremental algorithmic progress, fragmented regulatory landscape, labs compete independently

Plausible: Major algorithmic efficiency breakthrough (10x compute-equivalent gain) shifts capability timelines forward

Wildcard: Coordinated international framework emerges requiring capability eval gates before deployment; development pace constrained but more predictable

Why This Matters: The Pattern of Surprise

History repeatedly shows that our models underweight discontinuous change. The Soviet Union's collapse in 1991 caught intelligence agencies with sophisticated models off-guard — their frameworks couldn't process cascade dynamics. COVID-19's emergence and spread pattern surprised pandemic preparedness experts despite years of planning. More recently, the capability jumps in large language models surprised even many AI researchers on timeline expectations.

Wildcards aren't exotic edge cases — they're the parts of reality that our comfortable models systematically miss. This tool helps you explicitly map that territory rather than leaving it as a blind spot.

Working with the Cone

  • Be specific with assumptions: Not "AI progresses" but "Frontier model capabilities improve ~50% annually on standard benchmarks"
  • Prefer fewer changes per scenario: Changing 1-2 drivers keeps the causal story clean. If changing multiple, ensure each is independently justified and you can articulate why they'd shift together.
  • Plausible ≠ likely: These should feel realistic but not obvious — things that could reasonably happen and would meaningfully change your situation
  • Wildcards test boundaries: Low probability, high impact — what would break your current mental model? As futurist Wendy Schultz notes, focusing only on "plausible" futures can be maladaptive; reality doesn't care what we consider plausible.
  • Consider preposterous futures: Dator's Second Law states that any useful idea about the future must first appear ridiculous. The "preposterous" zone is where seeds of transformation often hide.
  • Name your scenarios: Give each a memorable title to reference in discussions (e.g., "Regulatory Reset", "Capability Surprise")
  • Define indicators: What early signals would suggest each scenario is starting to unfold?

The Futures Cone

The classic framework shows how possibility space expands as we look further into the future. Our scenarios map to these zones:

Projected — "The official future." Current trends extrapolated. Our Baseline.
Probable — Likely variations around the baseline.
Plausible — Could happen; realistic shifts in key assumptions. Our Plausible scenario.
Possible — Requires larger changes; stretches current models.
Preposterous — Seems absurd today; may contain seeds of transformation. Our Wildcard.

🎯 Essential Next Step: Set Up Monitoring

Scenarios are only useful if you can recognize which one is unfolding. The Indicators of Change tool helps you define early signals for each scenario — specific, observable markers that would tell you the plausible or wildcard is becoming reality. Without indicators, your cone analysis becomes a one-time intellectual exercise rather than an ongoing strategic asset.

What to Do With This Analysis

  • Validate your baseline: Are these actually your best estimates, or comfortable defaults? Use Key Assumptions Check to stress-test them. The assumptions you're least confident about deserve the most scrutiny.
  • Find robust strategies: Identify actions that work across both baseline and plausible worlds. These "no-regret moves" reduce exposure regardless of which future unfolds.
  • Set circuit breakers for wildcards: Don't plan wildcards in detail, but define decision points — specific triggers where you'd recognize the wildcard unfolding and need to pivot.
  • Connect to monitoring: Use the Indicators of Change tool to operationalize your scenarios. For each one, define 2-3 early signals you could actually observe. This transforms analysis into ongoing situational awareness.
  • Schedule reviews: Cone analyses decay. Revisit quarterly or when major drivers shift. Update assumptions based on what you've learned, and check which indicators have moved.
  • When to use Alternative Futures (2x2) instead: If you have two dominant uncertainties that interact in interesting ways, the 2x2 matrix approach may give you cleaner scenario structure. CoP is better for exploring multiple drivers; 2x2 is better for deep-diving two critical uncertainties.
  • Discuss with others: Share your scenarios with colleagues, domain experts, or stakeholders. Fresh perspectives often reveal blind spots in your assumptions. The best stress-testing comes from people who think differently than you do. Export your report and use it as a conversation starter.
