Uncertainty Cascades

Map interconnected uncertainties to understand compound risks and decision dependencies

What this tool does: Uncertainty Cascades helps you visualize how one uncertain outcome leads to further uncertainties, and how probabilities compound through these chains. This reveals which uncertainties deserve the most attention and where hedging strategies would be most valuable.

How it works:

  • Start with a root question — an uncertain event or decision you're facing
  • Add branches — the possible outcomes of that uncertainty, each with its probability
  • Chain further questions — for each outcome, what new uncertainties emerge?
  • Analyze — see which paths are most likely, where to hedge, and what to monitor

Probabilities multiply through chains: a 60% chance followed by a 50% chance yields 30% for that full path. This compounding often reveals that "likely" sequences are actually quite uncertain.

Example: Product Launch Decision

Root: "Will we get regulatory approval?" → branches to Yes (60%) and No (40%)

If Yes: "Will competitors respond aggressively?" → Yes (70%) / No (30%)

If No: "Can we modify for resubmission?" → Yes (80%) / No (20%)

Path "Approval → Aggressive Response" = 60% × 70% = 42% — your most likely scenario to plan for.

Step 3: Analysis

Once you've built your tree, analyze it to understand the probability distribution of outcomes and identify strategic opportunities.

What these analyses reveal:
• Pathways: All possible end-states ranked by probability — shows which futures to prioritize planning for
• Hedge Strategies: Decision points where multiple outcomes have meaningful probability — opportunities for robust actions that work across scenarios
• Trigger Points: Key questions whose answers will shift your probability landscape — what to monitor and when to revisit your strategy

What to Do With This Analysis

  • For high-probability paths: Develop detailed plans. Use Pre-mortem Analysis to stress-test them.
  • For hedge points: Identify "no-regret" actions — investments that pay off across multiple branches. Consider Safe-to-Fail Experiments to probe uncertain territory cheaply.
  • For trigger points: Set up monitoring, e.g. using the Indicators of Change tool. Define what evidence would update your probabilities, and what actions you'd take in each case.
  • Into your process: Add trigger points to your project dashboard or review cadence. When a key question resolves, return here to update probabilities and re-analyze.
  • For deeper analysis: Use the "Copy Report" button to export your cascade, then discuss with colleagues or paste into a Claude conversation for further exploration.
  • Companion tools: Consider pairing with Monte Carlo Simulation for quantitative modeling, Reference Class Forecasting to calibrate your probability estimates, or Key Assumptions Check to surface hidden assumptions in your branches.
