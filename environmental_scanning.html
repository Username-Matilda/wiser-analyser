What this tool does: Environmental Scanning makes explicit what remains implicit. Every situation is shaped by forces you may not consciously track—social currents, technological shifts, economic pressures, political tensions, psychological patterns. By systematically categorizing the drivers at play, you escape tunnel vision and see the fuller landscape of influence.

How it works:

  • Frame your situation — the decision, challenge, or dynamic you're analyzing
  • Scan each domain — use the prompting questions to identify relevant forces
  • Note metadata — capture your confidence, sources, and assumptions for each factor
  • Look for patterns — where is pressure building? What domains are under-scanned?

This is often a first step. The real value comes from exploring how these factors interact and might evolve—work that companion tools like Alternative Futures and Cone of Possibilities are designed for.

Example: Analyzing a Career Transition

Situation: "Should I leave my corporate job to join an early-stage AI safety startup?"

Factors might include: Social (family expectations, professional network effects), Economic (runway, opportunity cost), Technological (field trajectory, skills relevance), Psychological (risk tolerance, identity attachment), Political (regulatory landscape affecting the space).

Framing Tips:
  • Be specific about scope: "Our team's decision about X" rather than "the future of X"
  • Include time horizon: "...over the next 2 years" focuses the scan
  • Name your role: "As a [role], I'm analyzing..." clarifies which forces are relevant to you
  • State what's at stake: The importance helps prioritize which domains matter most

Scan Environmental Domains

For each domain, use the prompting questions to identify forces that might affect your situation. Capture what you know, what you're uncertain about, and where your information comes from.

🔗 Consider the Interplays

Factors rarely act in isolation. Before concluding your scan, consider how forces in different domains might amplify, dampen, or transform each other:

Reinforcing Loops

Which factors strengthen each other? (e.g., economic pressure + psychological stress → amplified urgency)

Competing Tensions

Which factors pull in opposite directions? (e.g., technological opportunity vs. regulatory constraint)

Hidden Couplings

Which factors might unexpectedly affect each other if one changes? (e.g., political shift → economic cascade)

Critical Dependencies

Which factors only matter if another factor holds? (e.g., market opportunity depends on regulatory stability)

Go deeper: For structured exploration of these interplays, use Alternative Futures (2×2) to test how different uncertainty resolutions create different scenarios, or Cone of Possibilities to map how the situation might evolve over time.

What to Do With This Analysis

  • Workshop with stakeholders: Share your scan with colleagues, advisors, or those who see the situation differently. Ask: "What am I missing? Which factors seem over- or under-weighted?"
  • Probe your assumptions: Each factor rests on beliefs about how the world works. Use the Key Assumptions Check tool to surface and stress-test these.
  • Explore interactions: Use Alternative Futures (2×2) to see how your key uncertainties might resolve into different scenarios. Use Cone of Possibilities for longer time horizons.
  • Set up monitoring: Use Indicators of Change to define observable signals that would tell you a factor is shifting. Environmental conditions change—your scan should too.
  • Deepen with Claude: Export your scan and paste it into a conversation. Ask for critical review: "What blind spots might I have? Which factors seem most likely to be wrong? Where should I focus my research?"
  • Revisit periodically: Environmental scans are snapshots. Set a reminder to update this scan—especially if a key factor has shifted or you've gained new information.
