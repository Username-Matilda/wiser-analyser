Fermi Estimation Worksheet

Break unknowable quantities into estimable pieces

The method: Decompose what you can't measure into what you can estimate. Multiply rough numbers, arrive at rough truth.

The art: Not all decompositions are equal. Carve reality at its joints — find components that are genuinely independent, that isolate the variables which actually drive the answer. A skilled decomposition reveals structure; a naive one obscures it.

The discipline: Each estimate carries uncertainty. Before finalizing, ask: What would make this off by 10x? If you can imagine it, widen your range.

📖 Worked Example: How many dentists in San Francisco?

Decomposition: Population (~880K) × visits per person per year (~1.5) ÷ visits a dentist handles per year (~2,000) = ~660 dentists.

Reality check: ~1,200 licensed dentists in SF. Within 2x — reasonable for back-of-envelope. Discrepancy explained by part-timers, specialists, commuter patients.

The joint we carved: Dentist productivity (1,500–2,500 visits/year) creates 60% variance alone. That's the variable that matters.

🧭 Principles for Good Fermi Estimates

  • Decompose until you can estimate: Keep breaking down until each component is something you can reasonably bound. "People per household" is estimable; "economic activity" is not.
  • Prefer multiplicative decompositions: Multiplying several rough estimates tends to be more robust than long addition chains. Errors often partially cancel.
  • Anchor on known quantities: Start with things you're confident about (populations, physical constraints, time units) and derive unknowns from them.
  • Use ranges, not points: A single number hides your uncertainty. Min/max bounds force you to think about what could vary.
  • Sanity check against reference classes: Does your answer make sense compared to similar things you know? If estimating a company's revenue, compare to similar companies.
  • Identify the crux: Which input, if wrong by 3x, would most change your answer? That's where additional research has highest value.
