Swing Weighting Calculator

Ground criteria weights in actual ranges rather than abstract importance

What this tool does: Swing weighting determines how much each criterion matters by asking a concrete question: "How valuable is improving from the worst available option to the best?" This grounds your weights in the actual improvements at stake, not abstract notions of importance.

Why it matters: When someone says "quality is more important than cost," that's meaningless without context. Is quality improving from 60% to 95%? Or from 90% to 92%? The same criterion can have vastly different weights depending on the actual range of options you face.

📊 Worked Example: Vendor Selection

Imagine you're choosing between three vendors. First, identify the worst and best values across your actual options:

Criterion Worst Best Unit The "Swing"
Cost $8M $2M $M Saving $6M
Delivery Time 18 months 6 months months Saving 12 months
Performance 60% 95% % Gaining 35 points

Step 1: Ask: "If all criteria were at their worst, which swing to best would I value most?" Suppose the $6M cost saving is most valuable—make that the reference swing at 100 points.

Step 2: Compare other swings. "Is saving 12 months worth 60% as much as saving $6M? Maybe 70 points." "Is gaining 35% performance worth 40% as much? Maybe 40 points."

Step 3: Normalize. Total = 100 + 70 + 40 = 210. Final weights: Cost = 48%, Delivery = 33%, Performance = 19%.

Have different stakeholders complete swing weighting independently, then use the Geometric Mean Aggregator to combine their weights mathematically.

🔍 Test Sensitivity

Weights are subjective. Use the MCDA Sensitivity Analysis to see how much the final ranking changes if weights shift—this reveals which weights truly matter.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Do these weights feel right? Would you defend them to stakeholders?
  • Did any swing surprise you by being more or less valuable than expected?
  • If the ranges changed (say, best cost became $3M instead of $2M), how would that shift your weights?
  • Are there criteria you initially thought were important but, given the actual ranges, matter less?
  • Would different stakeholders assign different scores? If so, whose perspective should dominate, and why?
Remember: These weights are specific to the ranges you defined. If your options change significantly—a new vendor appears, or requirements shift—revisit this analysis with updated ranges. The power of swing weighting is its grounding in reality; that grounding must stay current.
