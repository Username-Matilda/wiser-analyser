Indicators of Change

Define observable signals that reveal which future is unfolding

What this tool does: When you've identified possible futures (through scenario planning, alternative futures analysis, or strategic foresight), each scenario implies specific observable events. This tool helps you define those indicators in advance, plan how you'll monitor them, and export your analysis for ongoing use.

The principle: Scenarios aren't predictions—they're hypotheses. Good indicators let reality guide your understanding. By deciding what evidence would support each hypothesis before events unfold, you avoid the trap of retrofitting observations to preferred narratives.

Historical Example: The 2008 Financial Crisis

In retrospect, several indicators signaled the approaching crisis years in advance: rising subprime mortgage default rates, credit default swap spreads widening, declining home sales in overheated markets, and increasing leverage ratios at major banks. Those who tracked these signals systematically had time to prepare; most institutions that failed were not monitoring the right indicators—or weren't taking them seriously.

Your goal: identify what signals you should be watching now so you're not caught in hindsight bias later.

Scenarios & Their Indicators

For each scenario you're tracking, define specific observable events that would suggest it's materializing. Good indicators are concrete, time-bound where possible, and observable without requiring special access.

Ideas for Monitoring Your Indicators

The value of indicators depends on actually noticing when they trigger. Here are concrete ways to set up your "sensors":

📰 News & Media Monitoring
  • Google Alerts: Set up keyword alerts for specific terms, names, or phrases related to your indicators
  • RSS feeds: Follow relevant trade publications, regulatory bodies, or industry blogs via Feedly or similar
  • Newsletter subscriptions: Industry-specific newsletters often surface niche developments before mainstream coverage
  • Social media: Twitter/X lists for key experts; LinkedIn for company announcements; Reddit for community sentiment
📊 Data Sources
  • Government statistics: Bureau of Labor Statistics, SEC filings, regulatory disclosures, patent databases
  • Academic preprints: arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN for early research signals
  • Industry reports: Gartner, McKinsey, industry associations often publish tracking data
  • Financial indicators: Stock movements, commodity prices, bond yields, venture funding announcements
  • Alternative data: Satellite imagery, web traffic data, job postings (often signal company direction)
🤝 Human Networks
  • Conference attendance: Key events where developments get announced or discussed
  • Expert relationships: Regular check-ins with people who have relevant domain knowledge
  • Community participation: Slack/Discord communities, professional forums, working groups
  • Informal networks: Coffee chats with people at relevant organizations; alumni networks
🔄 Structured Review
  • Calendar reminders: Monthly or quarterly indicator reviews—actually schedule these
  • Team check-ins: Distribute monitoring responsibility; different people watch different domains
  • Trigger thresholds: Define in advance what level of signal warrants action or escalation
  • Collaborative workshopping: Discuss your indicators with colleagues, domain experts, or an AI assistant to identify gaps

Interpreting Your Indicators

  • Pattern over signal: A single indicator firing means little; multiple aligned indicators across a scenario strengthen the case
  • Absence is data: Expected indicators that don't appear are as informative as those that do—they suggest the scenario may not be materializing
  • Contradictions reveal complexity: Indicators from competing scenarios observed simultaneously suggest reality may be more nuanced than your models—or that scenarios need revision
  • Update honestly: When evidence contradicts your preferred scenario, take it seriously. Reality has no obligation to your hopes
