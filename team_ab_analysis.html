⚖️ The Adversarial Method: Truth emerges not from consensus but from contest. When two competing explanations merit serious consideration, we assign teams of equal capability to steelman each position — that is, to argue the strongest possible version of each hypothesis, not a weakened caricature. Each team argues not from preference but from rigor: what evidence supports this? What logic leads here?

This method works best when you're genuinely willing to discover that one hypothesis is stronger — even if you secretly hope the other wins. If you notice discomfort as evidence accumulates against your favored position, that's often diagnostic. By forcing genuine competition, we prevent the dominant narrative from steamrolling alternatives.

The Question

What are you trying to understand? Be specific. Not "Should we expand?" but "Should we open a second site in the next 18 months given current funding and team capacity?"

⚔️ Team A/Team B Principles

  • Equal treatment: Both teams get equal time, resources, and audience attention
  • Steelman, don't strawman: Each team argues the strongest possible version of their hypothesis
  • Assign against preference: Often, assign analysts to the hypothesis they don't initially favor
  • Outside assessment matters: Those not on either team provide the final evaluation
  • Document logic explicitly: Vague arguments are weak arguments made visible
  • Identify resolution criteria: What would prove one side right? That's your next research target

Outside Assessment

Step outside both teams. Evaluate the arguments with fresh eyes — ideally by someone who didn't argue either side.

💡 Tools for assessors: Consider using the Quality of Information Check to evaluate the evidence cited by each team (source credibility, methodology, biases), and the Key Assumptions Check to surface hidden premises in each team's reasoning that may not have been made explicit.

What To Do With This Analysis

  • If one hypothesis wins clearly: What actions follow? What would need to be true for you to act on it confidently?
  • If uncertainty remains: Pursue the "missing information" you identified. What's the cheapest test you can run?
